# Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Using Pure White and Pure Black

**What people do:** Set the background to `#FFFFFF` and text to `#000000`, assuming maximum contrast equals maximum luxury.

**Why it fails:** Pure white reads as a screen, not a material — it has no warmth, no depth, no surface. Pure black text on pure white is the default of every document ever rendered; it signals "default," which is the opposite of considered. Luxury is a material experience, and materials are never pure.

**Fix:** Warm ivory `#FAF7F2` ground, soft black `#262220` text. The 3-4% warmth is what makes the page feel printed on paper rather than displayed on a monitor.

---

## Mistake 2: Gold Everywhere

**What people do:** Add gold borders, gold text, gold buttons, gold icons — "luxury" interpreted as "lots of gold."

**Why it fails:** Gold is the accent, not the palette. When gold covers 30% of the page it reads as costume jewelry, casino signage, or a cheap "premium" template. The value of gold comes from its scarcity against neutrals.

**Fix:** One champagne element per page — typically the single CTA or one editorial detail. Everything else stays in the neutral register. If you catch yourself adding a second gold element, remove the first.

---

## Mistake 3: Cramming Content In

**What people do:** Keep the standard SaaS section list — hero, features, testimonials, pricing, FAQ, footer — and just make it look "clean."

**Why it fails:** Luxury pages are not compressed versions of SaaS pages. They carry 30-50% of the content of a typical landing page. A luxury brand doesn't need six sections; it needs three perfect ones. Density is the enemy of the whisper.

**Fix:** Cut sections until each remaining one has a full room of whitespace. One hero, one craft story, one collection, one footer is a complete luxury page. Add sections only when the brand genuinely has more to say.

---

## Mistake 4: Centered-Everything Symmetry

**What people do:** Center every headline, every image, every button — assuming symmetry equals elegance.

**Why it fails:** Total symmetry is static and corporate — it's the layout of a bank brochure. Real luxury composition is *calm asymmetry*: a headline offset-left, an image right-of-center, a micro label floating at a deliberate distance. The imbalance is what makes the page feel art-directed rather than templated.

**Fix:** Use the 12-column grid at 20% occupancy. Content in columns 2-7, images in columns 8-11. Center only the rare "statement" moment (a single word, a monogram) — and only once per page.

---

## Mistake 5: Flashy Motion

**What people do:** Add scroll-triggered parallax, magnetic buttons, marquee text, and spring physics to "make it feel premium."

**Why it fails:** Motion is the fastest way to break the illusion of a gallery. Galleries are still. Parallax and marquees are the vocabulary of tech startups and retail — they scream "website," which is exactly what luxury must not do. Bounce easing is the single most anti-luxury thing on the web.

**Fix:** Slow crossfades (1000-1800ms), one subtle image scale on reveal, hairline draw-ins. If a user can describe the animation, it's too much. When in doubt, remove it.

---

## Mistake 6: Stock Photography Energy

**What people do:** Use bright, saturated, smiling stock photos of "successful people" or generic luxury clichés (yachts, champagne glasses, city skylines).

**Why it fails:** Stock photography has a recognizable energy — over-lit, over-saturated, emotionally generic. It instantly dates the page and signals that the brand couldn't afford (or didn't care about) real art direction. The photography IS the luxury; it cannot be borrowed.

**Fix:** Art-directed photography with consistent soft light, real materials, and a coherent color grade. Even a single well-shot image of a material (linen, stone, brass) outperforms a stock photo of a yacht. If you must use stock, desaturate to 90%, warm the grade, and crop to 3:4 or 4:5.

---

## Mistake 7: Multiple Typefaces and Weights

**What people do:** Pair a display serif with a geometric sans, add a handwritten accent font, and use bold weights for emphasis.

**Why it fails:** Every additional typeface is a voice in the room. Luxury pages speak with one or two voices at most. Geometric sans (Futura, Montserrat) with serif is the 2015-startup pairing — instantly dated. Bold weights shout, and this style whispers.

**Fix:** One serif family (display + body) plus one sans for micro labels. Weights 300-500 only. Emphasis comes from italics, spacing, and size — never from weight alone.

---

## Mistake 8: Treating Whitespace as Leftover

**What people do:** Design the content first, then let the whitespace be whatever remains.

**Why it fails:** Whitespace designed as an afterthought is just empty space — it reads as unfinished. Luxury whitespace is *designed first*: the margins, gaps, and empty quadrants are the primary composition, and content is placed within them like objects in a room.

**Fix:** Design the negative space first. Set section padding to `clamp(6rem, 14vh, 12rem)` before placing any content. If a section feels empty, resist filling it — emptiness is the feature.
