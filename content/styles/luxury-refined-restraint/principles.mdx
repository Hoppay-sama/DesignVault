# Design Principles

## 1. WHISPER, DON'T SHOUT

Confidence without volume. Small type, vast space, restrained color, and photography that lets the subject breathe. The page never competes with the product — it frames it the way a museum frame holds a painting. If a viewer can say "that's a website," the style has failed; they should say "that's a beautiful room."

**What it prevents:** The default SaaS pattern of giant gradients, bold claims, and constant calls to action. Volume is what average design uses to compensate for lack of craft.

---

## 2. EVERY PIXEL IS CONSIDERED

Nothing accidental. Every margin, weight, image placement, and line-height is a deliberate choice with a reason. This principle is what separates "minimal" from "empty": minimalism removes; restraint curates. An empty page is lazy; a restrained page is a page where every remaining element has been vetted.

**What it prevents:** The "minimal but hollow" template where whitespace is a default rather than a decision.

---

## 3. SPACE IS THE LUXURY

Gallery spacing (py-48+ / clamp(6rem, 14vh, 12rem) section gaps) is the style's most expensive material. Luxury goods are priced by scarcity — of material, of craft, of space. A page that uses 20% of its canvas signals that the brand can afford to waste the rest. Density says "we need your attention"; emptiness says "we have your trust."

**What it prevents:** Information-dense layouts that feel like marketplaces rather than boutiques.

---

## 4. ONE ACCENT, ONE MOMENT

The palette is neutral; the single accent (champagne, oxblood, or emerald) appears once per page, at most. Scarcity creates value: the one gold element becomes the page's jewelry. If every other button were gold, the gold would mean nothing.

**What it prevents:** Multi-color systems that turn the page into a storefront sale.

---

## 5. MATERIAL OVER DIGITAL

Surfaces are ivory, stone, linen, brass — never glass, never glow, never gradient. Photography shows real materials in real light. The page should feel like it could be printed, bound, and shipped — a brochure you'd keep on a coffee table.

**What it prevents:** Glassmorphism, neon, 3D renders, and every other "digital-native" effect that dates a design to a specific technology trend.

---

## 6. CRAFT LIVES IN THE MICRO

True small caps, hanging punctuation, ligatures, tabular figures, hairline rules at exact 1px, tracking at 0.25em on labels — these are the details the eye can't name but the body feels. Luxury is the accumulation of details that are individually invisible and collectively unmistakable.

**What it prevents:** The "good enough" typography of default settings that makes every site feel typeset by the same machine.

---

## 7. TIMELESS OVER TRENDING

The style references 100 years of print and fashion heritage, not this year's design blog. If a technique can be dated to a specific year (glassmorphism 2022, bento grids 2023, brutalist type 2024), it does not belong here. The page should look as right in 2036 as it does today.

**What it prevents:** Seasonal design language that makes the brand feel temporary.
