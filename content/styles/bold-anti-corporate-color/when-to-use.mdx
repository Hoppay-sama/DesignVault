# When to Use

## Perfect For

| Context | Why This Style Works |
|---------|---------------------|
| **Streetwear & youth brands** | The loud, anti-establishment energy is native to street culture; saturated clashing colors signal a brand that doesn't play it safe |
| **Music festivals & concert promos** | Punk-poster energy, electric color and kinetic motion match festival lineups and live show posters |
| **Independent zines & print** | Risograph texture and hand-drawn marks are the literal language of indie publishing |
| **Record labels / indie musicians** | Raw, unpolished collage carries the underground credibility of DIY labels and artists |
| **Activist organizations** | Rebellion and provocation are the aesthetic — dissent reads instantly in clashing primaries |
| **Campaign microsites** | A one-off saturate poster-scape captures a cultural moment louder than any corporate template |
| **Youth / creator-facing apps** | Cultural moment tools, Gen-Z media and meme-adjacent products thrive on unpolished personality |

## Brand Personality Match

This style reinforces brands that are:
- **Loud** — energy is the feature, not a bug
- **Irreverent** — comfortable mocking the mainstream and corporate convention
- **DIY / unpolished** — imperfection and intentional mismatch are core values
- **Anti-corporate** — a direct rejection of minimal corporate polish
- **Culturally connected** — zine, streetwear and music-culture literacy matters

## Avoid When

| Context | Why It Fails |
|---------|-------------|
| **Corporate / B2B** | The anti-establishment energy reads as unprofessional to enterprise buyers |
| **Healthcare** | Clashing primaries and chaotic motion feel reckless, not reassuring |
| **Government / legal** | Defiance and rule-breaking are the risk profile, not the draw |
| **Finance / banking** | Saturated clash and zine collage scream volatility, not stability |
| **Luxury (understated)** | Refined restraint is the opposite of this aesthetic |
| **Long-form / content-heavy** | The collage layout and misalignment can't support dense editorial reading |
| **Brands needing trust** | Unpolished, saturated, intentionally misaligned image undercuts credibility |

## How to Decide

Ask: **"Does my brand want to feel loud, defiant and proudly unpolished — a punk poster, not a corporate deck?"**

If yes → Bold Anti-Corporate Color.
If no → Consider playful-interactive-maximal (if you want playful energy without the anti-corporate bite — or crave the jam-packed, game-like layout), typographic-brutalist (if the punch should come through type and grid), or flat-brutalist (if the defiance should stay austere and monochrome).
