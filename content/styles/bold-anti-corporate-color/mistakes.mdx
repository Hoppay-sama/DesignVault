# Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Over-Saturation Chaos

**What people do:** Put every primary in every section — five saturated colors, all overlapping, all fighting at once.

**Why it fails:** Clash without structure is noise, not rebellion. The style is loud because fields alternate: one dominant primary per section, with the clashing pair reserved for the accent. When every color shouts simultaneously, nothing is readable, the eye has no anchor, and the page reads as a glitch rather than a poster.

**Fix:** One dominant field color per section (acid yellow, hot pink, etc.). Reserve the clashing partner for text, stamps, and strokes. The clash should be an event, not the baseline.

---

## Mistake 2: Unreadable Contrast

**What people do:** Set body text in a low-contrast or mid-tone pairing — hot pink on electric orange, or cream at 60% opacity on lime green.

**Why it fails:** Body copy is the only place that reads comfortably in this style; if it can't be read, the page is decoration. The style is loud but it still needs ink-level contrast for anything longer than a headline. Translucent, veiled text is a softness this style refuses.

**Fix:** Body text at 90%+ opacity in ink black or warm cream — hard-stamped. Use the clashing combinations on short text: headlines, labels, stamps. Anything dense sits in ink on the field.

---

## Mistake 3: Decoration Without Hierarchy

**What people do:** Scatter stickers, rotations, and handwritten notes everywhere with no order of importance.

**Why it fails:** Collage needs a spine. The oversized condensed headline is the anchor; the scrawl, stamps, and stickers are annotation. When everything is decorated, nothing is emphasized — the eye can't tell the poster from the graffiti.

**Fix:** Keep one unmissable element per section (the giant headline or the hero stamp). Every handwritten note, rotation, and sticker should reference that anchor. If you can't name what a section is shouting, strip the decoration.

---

## Mistake 4: Centered, Safe Corporate Layout

**What people do:** Symmetric hero, type dead center, generous letter-spacing, everything optically balanced.

**Why it fails:** Perfect alignment is the corporate voice this style exists to reject. A centered headline with comfortable tracking reads "SaaS landing page" in any color. The layout is half the rebellion — if the structure is polite, the palette reads as decoration, not protest.

**Fix:** Type to one edge, force-ragged, rotated 1–3 degrees. Headlines may collide with margins and clip at the viewport. Raggedness is intentional, never accidental.

---

## Mistake 5: Softening the Palette

**What people do:** Dial saturation down, add a white-gray background behind the color, or pick two colors that "click."

**Why it fails:** The saturation IS the message. "Balanced" pairings and neutral backdrops are exactly the muted corporate vocabulary the style bans. Once the palette whispers, the design has surrendered — the rebellion is gone even if the fonts remain.

**Fix:** Full-strength primaries only. Pair fields that hurt: acid yellow with hot pink, cobalt with lime green, electric orange with cobalt. If two colors read as balanced, push them further apart.

---

## Mistake 6: Polished "Modern" Finish

**What people do:** Rounded cards, glassmorphism, soft layered shadows, gradient meshes, smooth 300ms fades.

**Why it fails:** Polish is the visual language of the establishment. Rounded glass and gradient meshes say "startup template," which is precisely the generic anti-corporate energy this style is meant to be — it's what every AI-generated site does by default. The finish must be print: hard edges, crop marks, ink crush.

**Fix:** Hard offset black borders and shadows, halftone/grain overlays, misregistered stamps. Corners stay sharp, shadows stay hard, surfaces look stamped on a risograph — never rendered in a glassmorphism shader.

---

## Mistake 7: Playing It Safe With Motion

**What people do:** Use standard ease-in-out fades and 300ms linear transitions, or go the other way with slow luxury drifts.

**Why it fails:** The motion voice is elastic, bouncy, chaotic. Standard easing makes the page feel like a corporate template that got dressed in punk clothes; slow drifts contradict the physical, thrown-object energy of the layout. Motion is part of the personality, not an afterthought.

**Fix:** Elastic easing with overshoot on reveals (200–500ms), squash-and-stretch on interactive elements, wobble on stamps and stickers. Bouncy, playful, slightly chaotic — but always quick enough to feel physical.
