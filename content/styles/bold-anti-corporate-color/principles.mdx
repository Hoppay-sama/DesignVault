# Design Principles

## 1. PERSONALITY OVER POLISH

Imperfection is the point. Nothing is retouched, tightened, or aligned for comfort. Misregistration, wobbly handwriting, clipped words, and rough edges are features — they prove a hand (or a stamp) was involved. The design admits it was made, quickly, on purpose, and it refuses to look like it came off a production line.

**What it prevents:** The sterile sheen of corporate minimalism. If a layout could pass a brand guidelines audit, it has failed — it needs friction, a bump, a scratch.

---

## 2. COLOR AS REBELLION

Saturated primaries that clash intentionally. Acid yellow (#D4FF00), hot pink (#FF2D95), electric orange (#FF5400), cobalt blue (#0049FF), lime green (#B6FF00). Full sections sit on solid primary fields; text, buttons, and stamps sit directly on them. Never pick two colors that "go together" — pair fields so the adjacent colors physically hurt in a good way.

**What it prevents:** Safe corporate blue, muted hedges, and gradient softening. The saturation is never dialed down for taste — a palette that refuses to whisper is the rebellion itself.

---

## 3. COLLAGE COMPOSITION

Overlapping, misaligned, mixed media. Layout is asymmetric and collage-like: elements layer over each other with hard edges, rotated 1–3 degrees, and clipped by the viewport. Stickers, stamps, and handwritten notes form a second layer on top of the fields. Intentional misalignment replaces the baseline grid.

**What it prevents:** Card-grid compositions that imply a corporate dashboard. The page is assembled like a zine spread — cut, pasted, overlapping — not laid out on a master grid.

---

## 4. ELASTIC ENERGY

Motion is bouncy, playful, slightly chaotic. Wobble, bounce, and elastic easing — elements overshoot, settle with a spring, and never glide on a linear curve. Transitions are quick enough to feel physical: 200–500ms with overshoot, not 1s+ cinematic drifts. Hover states squash and stretch like rubber.

**What it prevents:** Snappy corporate ease-in-out fades and slow, "premium" luxury motion. This style moves like a physical object thrown at a wall — springy, loud, alive.

---

## 5. PRINTED, NOT RENDERED

The surface reads as risograph, halftone, and screen-print — not as a GPU gradient. Texture carries the authenticity: halftone dots, grain, ink crush, misregistered stamps, hand-drawn illustration. Even full-bleed saturated fields get print artifacts that stop them from reading as digital screen-tone.

**What it prevents:** Default gradient meshes, glassmorphism, and glossy renders. If a surface could be mistaken for a SaaS hero image, the print texture is missing — the style must feel stamped, not rendered.
