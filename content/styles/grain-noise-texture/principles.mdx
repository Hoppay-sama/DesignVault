# Design Principles

## 1. TEXTURE IS THE DESIGN

Grain is not an effect applied at the end — it is the primary surface. Film grain, paper tooth, halftone dots, and risograph misregistration are the materials the page is made of, not decoration layered on top. The screen should feel like a physical object: printed on uncoated stock, handled, imperfect. If you remove the texture, the design has nothing left.

**What it prevents:** Building a clean digital page and dusting grain over it as garnish. Texture that is optional is texture that is decorative — here it is structural.

---

## 2. ANALOG IMPERFECTION

The style deliberately reintroduces the flaws digital design removed: misregistration, paper fiber, film jitter, slight color shifts. These imperfections are the point — they are a reaction against clinical retina perfection. The page should feel made, not rendered. Subtle unevenness in color blocks, a hairline that doesn't quite align, grain that shimmers like projected film — all of it says "human, tactile, real."

**What it prevents:** Sterile, machine-perfect output. Analog imperfection is the difference between a PDF and a printed piece you can feel.

---

## 3. MUTED TO GROUNDED

Colors are desaturated — warm neutrals (stone, sand, clay) or cool mineral tones (slate, graphite, chalk). Nothing glows, nothing shouts. The palette feels printed on uncoated stock: pigments that soaked into paper rather than light emitted from a screen. Contrast is slightly reduced — text is ink on muted stock, not pure black on white. This restraint is what makes the texture visible; saturated color would fight the grain for attention.

**What it prevents:** Neon or saturated palettes that belong on glossy screens. Muted color lets the texture and typography carry the design.

---

## 4. GENTLE MOTION ONLY

Movement is subtle and analog-feeling. Grain animates with slow 24fps jitter — the shimmer of projected film. Fades are slow and soft, like an exposure developing. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing slides with mechanical ease. Motion exists to reinforce the tactile, physical feeling — a print slowly catching the light — never to show off.

**What it prevents:** Snappy, springy, digital motion that breaks the analog spell. If the motion feels like a tech product, it fails the style.

---

## 5. SERIF OR WARM SANS

Typography pairs with both serif (editorial) and grotesque (modern) faces, but always with slightly reduced contrast — text is never pure black on white. The type should feel set in ink: warm, slightly softened, at ease on a textured surface. Editorial serifs for heritage and narrative, warm grotesques for a modern but human voice.

**What it prevents:** Glossy, hyper-optimized, high-contrast digital type. The type must sit on paper, not float on a screen.
