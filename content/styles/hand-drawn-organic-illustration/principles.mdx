# Design Principles

## 1. THE HAND IS THE SIGNAL

Visible imperfection proves human authorship. Wobbly lines, slightly-off shapes, and visible stroke variation are not flaws — they are the entire point of the style. The hand is a counter-signal to AI-generated perfection: a declaration that a human made this. When a user sees a slightly wobbly circle, their brain registers "a person drew this" — that recognition is the aesthetic. Not childish, not sloppy — sophisticated illustration with intentional imperfection.

**What it prevents:** Retouching illustrations to be "cleaner," straightening lines, or redrawing shapes in vector perfection. The moment the hand disappears, the style dies.

---

## 2. MEDIA AS TEXTURE

The medium is visible: pencil grain, watercolor bleed, ink splatter, marker streaks, eraser marks — all sitting on paper texture underneath. Color isn't flat; it's painted. Lines aren't uniform; they're pressed harder in places. These media textures do the work that gradients and shadows do in other styles — they give depth, warmth, and authenticity. The texture declares the material, and the material declares the process.

**What it prevents:** Flat fills, clean uniform strokes, or digital gradients that look printed. The style needs to feel like it could exist on a physical page.

---

## 3. ORGANIC FLOW

Layout follows illustration, not grid. The drawing dictates the composition — text flows around it, containers wobble around it, elements break out of bounds. Nothing is pinned to a baseline grid. The page should feel like a spread in a sketchbook: drawing first, then notes fitted into whatever space remains. The organic flow makes every viewport size feel like a custom composition rather than a rescaled template.

**What it prevents:** Boxed-in layouts, rigid alignment, and the urge to "tidy up" the composition into a conventional grid.

---

## 4. WARM, EARTHY COLOR — OR BOLD MARKER

Two valid palettes: warm and earthy (terracotta, ochre, sage, cream, charcoal) for artisanal, calm, natural brands — or bold marker colors (cobalt, vermillion, lemon) for playful, expressive, children-oriented work. Both are slightly imperfect in application: color bleeds past line edges, washes are uneven, marker streaks are visible. Color is applied like a hand applies it — rarely perfectly inside the lines.

**What it prevents:** Perfectly flat, evenly-distributed color fields. Machine-perfect fills read as printed, not drawn.

---

## 5. NOTHING MECHANICAL

Motion is gentle and organic — SVG draw-on animations (stroke-dasharray), wobble and breathing effects, lines that wiggle in on load. Nothing snaps, nothing loops with mechanical precision, nothing moves like a physics simulation. Even interactive elements feel hand-actuated: buttons wobble slightly on hover, illustrations sway like a leaf. The motion should feel like the drawing is alive — not like a tech demo.

**What it prevents:** Spring physics, 200ms snap transitions, and any motion that feels engineered. If the animation looks like it came from a component library, it's wrong.
