# Reference Gallery

## Live Sites

### Mailchimp (mailchimp.com)
- **What works:** Signature illustrated mascot and hand-drawn spot illustrations across a consumer product, proof that illustration-led design scales to enterprise software
- **URL:** mailchimp.com
- **Study:** How illustration is used as the primary brand signal while UI stays functional — and how wobbly, imperfect mascot art humanizes a SaaS brand

### Sketchbook (sketchbook.com)
- **What works:** Illustration-forward marketing that treats the canvas, pencil, and brush as hero elements
- **URL:** sketchbook.com
- **Study:** How tool texture (stroke, grain, bleed) is rendered as surface and mood rather than decoration

### Studiocanoe (studiocanoe.com)
- **What works:** Editorial illustration studio with sketchy line work, hand-lettering, and painterly texture
- **URL:** studiocanoe.com
- **Study:** How a studio site lets each illustration break the container — layouts follow the art, not the grid

### The New Yorker Cartoons (newyorker.com/cartoons)
- **What works:** The definitive editorial cartoon aesthetic — ink line, loose hatching, one-line wit
- **URL:** newyorker.com/cartoons
- **Study:** How confident, imperfect ink strokes carry humor and narrative with minimal elements

### Aardman Animations (aardman.com)
- **What works:** Clay-mation craft brand whose site uses hand-made texture, warm palette, and playful character design
- **URL:** aardman.com
- **Study:** How handcrafted imperfection builds a warm, artisanal brand identity

### Etsy (etsy.com)
- **What works:** Marketplace of handmade goods whose brand language leans on hand-drawn marks, paper texture, and craft warmth
- **URL:** etsy.com
- **Study:** How "handmade" is communicated through color (warm neutrals) and sketchy visual language

## Key Visual References

### Line & Mark
- **Wobbly line work** — inconsistent stroke weight, deliberate double-strokes and overshoots
- **Ink splatter & drips** — controlled mess as accent, never random noise
- **Hatching/cross-hatching** — editorial shading from cartoons and sketchbooks
- **Eraser marks & pencil grain** — visible media texture proving the hand

### Color References
- **Warm, earthy** — terracotta, ochre, sage, cream, charcoal as the base
- **Bold marker accents** — cobalt, vermillion, lemon used sparingly like a marker thrown into a sketch
- **Paper background** — cream/off-white canvas underneath everything

### Typography References
- **Handwritten display** — Caveat, Patrick Hand for headlines and labels
- **Clean sans body** — neutral, readable, lets the hand-drawn type and illustration sing
- **Hand-lettered accents** — pull quotes, margin notes, and dividers drawn rather than typeset

### Motion References
- **SVG draw-on** — stroke-dasharray animations that "sketch" lines onto the page
- **Wobble/breathing** — gentle 1-2% scale or rotation, organic easing, nothing mechanical
- **Ink bleed reveals** — watercolor washes fading in slowly

## Screenshot Notes

> When capturing references for this style, look for:
> - The illustration (must be hand-drawn in appearance — wobbly, textured, imperfect)
> - The color palette (must be warm earthy base with bold marker accents)
> - The texture (must show paper grain, pencil, watercolor bleed, or ink)
> - The layout (must follow the illustration — elements breaking out of containers)
> - The motion (must be gentle, organic, draw-on — never mechanical)
> - The typography (must mix handwritten display with clean body sans)
> - The overall mood (must feel human, warm, artisanal — not machine-made)
