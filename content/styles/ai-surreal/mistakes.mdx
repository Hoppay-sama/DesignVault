# Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Using Realistic Photography

**What people do:** Use stock photography or realistic images instead of AI-generated surreal imagery.

**Why it fails:** The entire aesthetic is built on synthetic, machine-imagined visuals. Realistic photography feels wrong — it's too grounded, too real. The dreamlike quality comes from imagery that could only exist in latent space.

**Fix:** Use AI-generated imagery (Midjourney, DALL-E, Stable Diffusion). The images should have impossible materials, hybrid surfaces, and scale ambiguity. If it could be a photograph, it's not surreal enough.

---

## Mistake 2: Hiding AI Artifacts

**What people do:** Over-process AI imagery to remove artifacts, weird geometry, and texture glitches.

**Why it fails:** The artifacts are the signature. They say "this was made by a machine, and that's the point." Removing them makes the imagery look like attempted photography — which is the wrong aesthetic. The uncanny valley is the aesthetic.

**Fix:** Feature the artifacts. Don't fix weird hands, impossible geometry, or texture glitches. These are design elements, not bugs. The artifacts are what make the imagery feel machine-imagined.

---

## Mistake 3: Fast, Snappy Motion

**What people do:** Use 300ms transitions and spring physics because "modern web design is snappy."

**Why it fails:** This is the slowest-moving style. Everything drifts, morphs, and breathes at 1000ms+ durations. Fast motion feels mechanical and breaks the dreamlike atmosphere. A 300ms fade-in is jarring in this context.

**Fix:** All transitions 800ms minimum. Drift animations 8-12s. Breathing animations 6s. Morphing 12s. The motion should feel like watching a dream — slow, continuous, unhurried.

---

## Mistake 4: Text-Heavy Layouts

**What people do:** Add headlines, subheadlines, body text, and labels to every section.

**Why it fails:** The imagery carries 90% of the visual weight. Text is metadata — a whisper over the visual roar. More text competes with the imagery and breaks the dreamlike atmosphere. The page should feel like an art gallery, not a blog post.

**Fix:** Limit text to: one moderate headline per section, 1-2 sentences of body text, and minimal labels. If you need more text, the imagery isn't strong enough — get better AI images.

---

## Mistake 5: Uniformly Saturated Palette

**What people do:** Use fully saturated colors throughout because "the imagery should be vivid."

**Why it fails:** The dreamlike quality comes from the contrast between desaturated base and rare saturated accents. If everything is saturated, the effect is lost — it just looks like colorful digital art. The desaturation is what makes the accents feel like vivid memories.

**Fix:** Desaturated pastel base (80-90% saturation). Rare saturated accents within the base — a vivid magenta flower in a dusty pink field. The saturation should be surprising, not constant.

---

## Mistake 6: Sharp, Geometric UI

**What people do:** Use sharp-cornered cards, precise borders, and geometric UI elements.

**Why it fails:** Everything in this style morphs, drifts, and blends. Sharp geometric UI feels mechanical and breaks the dreamlike atmosphere. The UI should feel like part of the same dream — organic, morphing, alive.

**Fix:** Morphing border-radius on UI elements. Iridescent, oily surfaces instead of flat colors. No sharp corners — everything has organic, shifting shapes. The UI should feel like it was generated by the same machine that made the imagery.

---

## Mistake 7: Solid Color Backgrounds

**What people do:** Use solid color backgrounds between image sections because "the page needs structure."

**Why it fails:** The imagery IS the background. Solid color sections break the dreamlike continuity — they feel like waking up from a dream. The page should be a continuous visual experience, not a series of images separated by solid colors.

**Fix:** Full-bleed imagery in every section. Between sections, use morphing crossfades — one image slowly becoming another. No solid color backgrounds. The only "background" is the imagery itself.
